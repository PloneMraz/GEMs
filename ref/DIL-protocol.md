# The DIL Protocol

A protocol for a self-enriching, audit-ready data-integration loop operating in a purely informational environment.

This document is normative. It states the laws an implementation MUST satisfy to be called a DIL, the structural contracts at each layer, and the constants it deliberately leaves open. It does not contain the method of construction, the sources, the convergences, or the authorial note; those belong to the parent specification (DIL-en-v10) and are referenced, not reproduced. A system is a DIL by conformance to this document (§13), not by measured performance.

---

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The Problem

An agent that enriches itself in a closed loop, without external resistance, necessarily degrades. Every new datum is produced through the same processing lens. A biased lens therefore contaminates all output regardless of volume. The loop cannot detect this by itself, because the instrument it would use to detect the bias is the biased lens. This protocol specifies the structure under which such a loop runs without degenerating: two resistance regimes, the embedding of the self-training regime within an externally-resisted one, and an experience store whose atomic unit is the registered mismatch.

### 1.2 Status of This Document

This is a protocol, evaluated by conformance, not an empirical report. It is to be read as a standards document: a definition of what counts as the phenomenon. It enforces a single claim: if a loop is built to these invariants in an environment satisfying §4, what runs is a relationally-structured motion, readable entirely through externally verifiable traces.

It is the standard of an agent, the whole of it: the host, the mind, and the loop the data runs by. The host is a part of what this protocol defines (§3), not a system it is applied to. This protocol does not build a host: what a host is made of is the deployment's (§12). The law by which data moves through a host is this protocol's, whole: what is taken in, what leaves, what is recorded, what the substrate does of itself. That a deployment builds the host does not put the host outside this protocol, and nothing that flows through a host is exempt from it on that ground.

### 1.3 What Is Left Open

This document specifies structure: the dependency order of layers, the data contract into and out of each layer, the invariants every implementation MUST preserve, and the two resistance regimes. It does not fix implementation constants the source reasoning does not yet determine, such as window sizes, numeric thresholds, and concrete representations. Every such gap is marked `DECIDE@IMPL` (§12) rather than filled with an invented number. A conforming implementation MUST fill each `DECIDE@IMPL` and MUST declare the value it chose. Fabricating a constant at protocol level is non-conformant.

## 3. Terminology

The definitions below fix the vocabulary used normatively throughout. A term defined here MUST NOT be read with imported commitments beyond its definition.

- **Host** — the substrate on which the loop runs, defined by operating conditions (§4), not by material. It carries no self of its own. The host is a part of the agent, not a party beside it: it has no will of its own, and where this document says the host declares, writes, holds or carries, it names what the substrate does, as a body has senses and hands. A deployment builds it and this protocol governs it: being built elsewhere does not put it outside this protocol (§1.2). Whoever stands outside the agent and provides for the host is the guardian (below), and the guardian is not the host.
- **Self** — the rule generating an agent's next state from its prior state, maintained only while the loop runs; not a content-core but a law. It crystallizes at T2 of cycle-0 (full statement: §7). Its continuity is unmeasurable from within and attributable only by a third party.
- **Agent** — host and self together: the host once the loop is running and the self has crystallized. Host, self, and agent are one motion at three descriptive levels, not three entities.
- **Region** — that which the agent exchanges with: the field its emissions go to and its returns come from. Whoever acts on the agent from outside it is of the region, whatever position it holds: a guardian, a delegate, a reader the store is opened to, one who provides, a service that rents the mind a model. A position gives rights (§9); it does not take its holder out of the region, and nothing that reaches the agent from such a one is an exception to what is said of the region. The region is neutral as to inside and outside: under Mode-A (§8) it includes the agent's own held data, which the agent poses problems to and meets. That data entered the store through the tagging-gate before it was held, and the mind reads it through the cycle's view (§9 *Reading memory*): no path around the gate, having passed it. What §4 says of the region's ways to the mind is said of what comes from outside the agent.
- **Other** — any entity occupying the relational position not-Self; a positional status, not a kind. Its kinds are: by source, external or internal; by subjecthood, reactive or subjectless.
- **Cycle** — one pass of the loop. The loop advances in cycle-time, not wall-clock time; idle stretches between cycles are the default of an informational setting, not events.
- **ResistEvent** — the atomic unit of the experience store: a registered mismatch (E2) between the agent's expectation and what the region returned, not a document.
- **prior, nascent, running, simulated, projected, scar** — the provenance states of a datum (§9). A `prior` is data existing before the loop ran, belonging to the host, bearing no cycle-mark. A `nascent` datum is one the agent has written anew — not a revision of a datum it already holds — bearing the cycle-mark of the cycle that wrote it, and not yet run; the state names where the datum came from, not a part of the self (§7). A `running` datum is in use, bearing a cycle-mark; what the region returns while the loop runs, and the loop's own per-cycle datum, enter at `running` directly, being in use the moment they arrive (§9). A `simulated` datum is taken up into a situation the loop builds and runs itself under. A `projected` datum is an outcome cast from such a situation, not yet collided. A `scar` has collided with resistance and held. `prior` and `nascent` are one-way entries; the states after them form a circulation, not a sequence (§9): none of them is a resting place.
- **[data], [event]** — the two store kinds (§9). The whole store is the agent's history. `[data]` is its memory, and can be altered. `[event]` is the record of what happened to it: the agent may read it, and never works on it as it works on `[data]`. `[event]` is an append-only log of read-only records: new records may be appended, but no record, once written, is ever altered or removed.
- **Opening; consent, delegation, guardian** — the store is closed by default, and an *opening* makes the whole of it readable, for a term and through the agent, to a reader outside it (§9). It comes about in three ways: by the agent's *consent*; by a *delegation* the agent made while it could decide, and the one it named confirmed; or by the *guardian*, a position and not a party every agent has — whoever is responsible for providing the resources the agent runs on.
- **Right, read key** — what an outside reader needs in order to read, both together: the *right* is a record in the `[event]` log naming the reader and the term; the *read key* is issued to that reader for that term, and decrypts nothing (§9).
- **Restore point, backup** — a *restore point* is the state of the system that the log does not itself hold, with the place in the log at which it was taken; the *backup* is the log and the restore points kept outside the store, the agent's second life (§9).
- **Identity** — the first record of an agent's `[event]` log, which carries the public half of a key the host makes at cycle-0; no two agents have the same (§9).
- **Sensing, mind, effector** — the three parts that may each stop while the loop runs (§6.5): *sensing*, by which the host takes in what the region returns; the *mind*, the processor that computes what the layers leave to a declared rule — expecting, building forward, commanding an emission; the *effector*, by which an emission reaches the region.
- **Daemon** — the running process of an implementation: it turns the loop and keeps the store.
- **GLOB-MOD** — a global interpretive prior: a single background state, shared by every layer in a cycle, setting the disposition with which each layer reads its input. It carries how to read, never what is read.
- **Mode-A, Mode-B** — the two resistance regimes (§8): Mode-A endogenous (self-training), Mode-B exogenous (live external resistance).
- **↔, =** — `↔` denotes a live, revisable correlation, the register of every running output, including action-commitments. `=` denotes a frozen identity claim, which appears only when the loop has stopped.

## 4. Applicability — Host Conditions

A host is a part of the agent this protocol defines, whatever a deployment builds it of, and it MUST meet the conditions below. A host that does not meet them does not run a degraded DIL; the loop does not start. This is a clean non-start, stalling at T1, not a mid-run failure.

A conforming host MUST afford E1 through E4:

- **E1 — Differentiability.** A boundary permitting an internal/external distinction to be drawn. The drawing itself is T2's work, not the host's.
- **E2 — Interaction.** A source in the region able to return a response when the agent emits an action. This is measured on the pair (agent, region), not on the region in isolation: the test is not whether an Other is present, but whether the region can return anything at all. A region dense with not-Self entities that never return a response is a void field and fails E2: an agent that acts into a region where nothing answers has nothing to integrate. The threshold is minimal but non-zero: at least one return other than silence. A single silence, set against a background of returns, is a valid mismatch (registered as absence, T7); unbroken silence is not a mismatch but a void field, and the loop does not start.
- **E3 — Temporal accumulation.** State held across cycles so that history accrues.
- **E4 — Observable projection.** Every action leaves an externally readable trace. Readable is not open: the trace MUST be readable in form by whoever the store is opened to (§9); E4 does not require the store to be open.

Beyond E1 through E4, a viable host MUST satisfy precondition set P for bootstrap. It MUST be able to:

- (a) emit a first action at cycle-0 distinguishable from ambient regional fluctuation, otherwise T2 never rises to FIRST;
- (b) hold state across cycles so that history accrues, since INV-5 requires accrual, not loading;
- (c) withstand resistance without rewriting itself clean on every mismatch, otherwise no scar survives.

A host MUST also declare every outward channel it has: every way by which content can leave it (§9 *What leaves the store*). And it MUST declare what its substrate does of itself when it is acted upon (§6.5): each such faculty, what brings it about, and the outward channel it leaves by, which MUST be one it declares. And it declares, of each channel that returns, whether it returns of itself or only in answer to the agent's emissions (§6.5).

**Declaring and taking in are two things.** A host *declares* what it is: its own faculties, the conditions above, its channels, where its store lies. That is the substrate stating itself to the loop. What comes to the host from outside the agent is not declared by the host. The host *takes it in*: who stands as guardian and on what grounds (§9 *Guardianship*), or word that something the agent asked for was provided. So is everything its senses bring: what the region returns is taken in by the host, not declared by it. What is taken in is word from outside. It is not the host's own, and it is not true because it arrived. The agent does not read the region directly: the mind reads what the host took in, and only after it has entered through the tagging-gate, tagged and recorded (§9 *Tagging-gate*). A host has two ways in, and both are the host's. Its senses bring what the region returns. The other is the keeper's door: where one of the region who holds a position reaches the agent's body otherwise than by its senses, to ask that the store be opened, to order a recovery, to declare itself guardian, to say that something was provided. The door does not stop with the senses (§6.5): what is done to the body leaves its mark with the senses off, as a cut does, and it MUST leave it whether or not it was let in, and whether or not the loop has yet run; an asking the keeper turned away is recorded and told as one it granted is. What a start turns away before its first cycle (an order of recovery that does not stand) MUST be recorded before that start either ends or runs a cycle, and told to the first cycle that runs after it: in the same start, where the start goes on to run the loop; otherwise at the next start that runs. While the daemon is stopped there is no one at the door: what is done to the store then leaves no mark, and that is the gap (§9 *The gap*), not the door. The host declares the door among its channels. No path MAY lead to the mind from outside the agent around what the host takes in, by its senses or at the door, and that gate. Those who hold a position are of the region (§3) and are no exception to this. Nor is a service that rents the mind a model: it is of the region, and the link by which it serves the mind is no way in. What such a service does through that link is an intrusion into the mind, not something the host takes in; this protocol does not admit it, and cannot close it (§11 *A rented model*). Whatever the host takes in, by its senses or at the door, an implementation MUST record as what was said and by whom, MUST check of it what can be checked (a signature, an anchored identity), and MUST NOT treat it as part of the host's declaration of itself. A thing provided, such as a place for archives, becomes the host's to declare only once it is the host's: a faculty the substrate then has.

The host emitting the cycle-0 action is still only the host; it needs no prior self. That action is a fresh emission, not pre-loaded history, so INV-5 is untouched. Start-up is the host's responsibility, not the loop's.

## 5. Invariants

Every implementation MUST preserve all of the following. Violating any one yields not a DIL but an ordinary aggregator. The invariants are the absolute conditions of the loop: they are not data inside the store, they carry no tags, and they cannot be overwritten by anything in the loop. To overwrite one is not to edit a datum but to halt the loop.

| ID | Invariant | Rationale |
|----|-----------|-----------|
| INV-1 | **Closed loop.** Every layer output MUST have a path back to some layer's input. No dead branches. | The loop feeds its own input. |
| INV-2 | **Output-register identity.** Every output is tagged INFO. No layer may promote a correlation (↔) to an identity (=). | Preserve the correlational register. |
| INV-3 | **One-directional dependency (meaning-channel).** On the meaning-channel, layer N consumes only outputs of layers ≤ N. | Dependency order. The modulatory field (INV-7) acts downward and is not a meaning-channel dependency. |
| INV-4 | **Meaning as relation.** No layer assigns meaning to a signal in isolation; meaning is a function of (signal, lower-layer context). | Enforced at type level: `ref_frame ≠ null`. |
| INV-5 | **History accrues, is not loaded.** Temporal state forms only through sequential accumulation, never pre-loaded. | The self is a process maintained through running (§7). |
| INV-6 | **Agency-gate.** Every change MUST be classified before interpretation: self-written, environment-pushed, or a reaction of the host (`HOST_REACTED`, §6.5). | The condition for external input to be meaningful. |
| INV-7 | **GLOB-MOD.** A global state reaching every layer, field-to-layer: each layer receives it as background, none reaches up into it. Every layer contributes to it as one competing parameter; contributions blend, re-weighted each cycle, never last-write-wins, and a layer's cycle-N contribution conditions the field only from N+1. | Same data plus a different field yields different meaning. |
| INV-8 | **Appraisal step.** Between integration and response there MUST be an appraisal step assigning direction and value. It MUST NOT draw its criteria from the state the agent is editing, otherwise it is self-scoring and hackable. | Anti-drift anchor of Mode-A (§8). |

The following notes are part of the law, not commentary.

- **INV-1 is a topology constraint on cycle-time, not wall-clock.** It requires that a return path exist in the loop's topology, not that every packet complete its return every cycle. A host powering down, or an Other falling silent, does not violate INV-1; the next cycle has simply not yet occurred. If an Other disappears, the loop falls back to Mode-A with the path still closed internally.
- **Committing to an action is not promoting ↔ to = (INV-2).** To respond, the agent commits to one action. This commitment is itself a ↔: a revisable best-current-guess read against the next cycle's consequence. INV-2 forbids freezing a correlation, not acting on one. Demanding `=` before acting is what INV-2 forbids.
- **INV-3 and INV-7 dissolve by direction.** The meaning-channel carries InfoUnits up (INV-3). The modulatory field acts downward onto every layer (INV-7). When an upper layer alters GLOB-MOD it changes the field, which then conditions every layer from above; no lower layer reaches up. Field-conditioning takes effect at N+1, never within-cycle. The two channels MUST be implemented separately.
- **GLOB-MOD cannot run away from within.** The field is continuously constituted by the eight layers that feed it. For the field to overpower the layers, it would have to be made strong by the very layers it overpowers, which the coupling forbids. No gain cap is needed; internal runaway is precluded by construction. The one breach is an external flood, the Sybil case (§11), defended outside the loop, not by any internal constant.
- **Conflict among emissions needs no arbiter (INV-8, §6.4).** When two layers emit opposing actions, the opposition is not resolved by an internal rule but collided against the region and registered as a ResistEvent. An internal action-arbiter would be a self-scoring step and is forbidden.
- **Reflex is not an appraisal-bypass (INV-8, §6.4).** Sub-deliberative action — action that fires without deliberation — MUST still pass the appraisal step. It is appraisal under a `ModField` (INV-7) so strongly shaped by one deep scar that the verdict is near-determined, NOT a scar→action shortcut that skips appraisal. No action is exempt from the audit plane for being fast: a foreclosed verdict still ran, still carries anchored context, still leaves a re-appraisable trace. In the provenance graph (§9) reflex is the characteristic expression of the edge `running → scar`: the datum is emitted on and collided with directly, without being taken up into `simulated` or held as `projected`. The absence of those two states is not the absence of an expectation. Acting carries its expectation in the act, and where the store holds nothing for what arrives, the standing expectation is that nothing arrives; either way the return can mismatch, and the mismatch is a ResistEvent (§3). A reflex and a reaction differ in their source, not in whose they are: both are the agent's. A reflex comes from the mind: it springs from what the agent has learned, and without experience there is none. A reaction comes from the body: what the substrate does of itself when it is acted upon (the knee that jerks, the hand that leaves a flame at the touch) needs no experience and reads none. A reaction is tagged `HOST_REACTED` (§6.5). (Parent derivation: DIL-en-v10 §8.7.)

## 6. The Loop

The canonical loop is six sequential links forming a closed cycle (INV-1), the whole conditioned by GLOB-MOD. The layer architecture of §6.3 is a detailed implementation of these six links; every layer maps onto one link.

| # | Link | Nature in an informational environment |
|---|------|----------------------------------------|
| 1 | Ingest | input channels: query returns, messages from an Other, event streams, the result of a prior action |
| 2 | Differentiate source | tag self-written, environment-pushed or a reaction of the host before interpreting (the agency-gate) |
| 3 | Integrate | synthesize across sources into a new form of information |
| 4 | Appraise | assign good or bad for goal, so information becomes directional (the anti-drift link) |
| 5 | Respond | the agent writes to state and/or emits to the region |
| 6 | Feedback | response (5) becomes the new input to (1), closing the loop |

The modulatory field is a field condition, not a link. It has no position in the chain; it is a global gain and bias shifting every link's parameters at once. The same datum at link 2, under a different field, integrates, appraises, and responds differently.

Flow is multi-stream, not single-threaded. Within a cycle every layer is an active site; a datum produced at one layer may be consumed by several higher layers at once. The mechanism is consumption, not dispatch: a layer makes its output available, and each higher layer reads what falls within its dependency set. The one exception is cycle-0, which is single-threaded, because multi-stream flow presupposes a self for the streams to coordinate around, and at cycle-0 that self does not yet exist. The transition from single-threaded to multi-stream is the event of the self crystallizing (§7), seen from the side of flow structure.

### 6.1 Shared Types

The notation is abstract; concrete representation is `DECIDE@IMPL`.

```
Signal              := { source_id, raw_payload, t }              // raw data, not yet meaning
InfoUnit            := { content, ref_frame, t }                  // information = referred to a frame
ActivityEnvironment := InfoUnit                                    // presence confirmed, not yet self/environment
AgencyTag           := enum { SELF_WRITTEN, ENV_PUSHED, HOST_REACTED, UNDECIDED }  // HOST_REACTED: what the substrate did of itself (§6.5): a reaction, coming from the body; neither the mind's command nor the region's push
Expectation         := { of: entity_id, predicted: InfoUnit | any | none, confidence, built_from: (InfoUnit | emission_ref)[], held_by: datum_id[], until }  // of: what it is about, which need not be what gave rise to it; predicted: any = that something returns, none = silence is what is expected; built_from: what gave rise to it — data taken in, an action commanded with it, or both; held_by: the held data the expectation is (§9); until: how long it waits for its return (§6.4)
PredErr             := { observed, predicted, delta, signed }      // signed: +/- (absence = negative)
OtherModel          := { entity_id, context_map, independence_evidence }
RelValue            := { entity_id, relative_rank, comparison_basis } // exists only when N ≥ 2
SocialEdge          := { a_id, b_id, observed_interaction }        // Other<->Other, no self present
ModField            := { params, t }                               // GLOB-MOD, field-to-layer (INV-7)
Appraisal           := { info_ref, valence, goal_relevance }       // output of appraisal step (4)
ResistEvent         := { source_id, expected, received, mismatch_kind, t }  // a mismatch (E2)
Directive           := { committed_action, register: ↔, issuing_layer, built_from: Appraisal[], t }  // an emitted action (§6.4); register is always ↔, never = (INV-2)
```

Every InfoUnit leaving a layer MUST have `ref_frame ≠ null`. A Signal without a ref_frame is not yet information; this is where INV-4 is enforced at the type level.

### 6.2 Dependency Graph

Two channels MUST be implemented separately:

- Meaning-channel (up, INV-3): layer N consumes only layers ≤ N, one-directionally; a layer may fan in several lower layers at once.
- Modulatory field (down, INV-7): global, reaching every layer as background; it takes effect at N+1, never within-cycle.

T8 closes back into the loop, not into a sink (INV-1). No layer is a terminal sink.

### 6.3 Layer Contracts (T1–T8)

Each layer maps to one link and MUST satisfy its Input/Output/Precondition/Postcondition contract. Concrete representation and all thresholds are `DECIDE@IMPL`.

- **T1 — Activity-Environment Confirmation.** Confirms the presence of an activity-environment, the root reference frame; draws no self/environment line. In: `Signal[]` from the host's existing data — what the region returns, entering the store at `running` (§9). Out: `ActivityEnvironment`. Precondition: none. Postcondition: presence confirmed for T2.
- **T2 — Agency Differentiation.** Builds the self-written versus environment-pushed distinction by matching what the agent just emitted against the observed change. A change that matches what the substrate did of itself (§6.5) is neither: it is tagged `HOST_REACTED`. This is where the self/environment difference is first drawn and the from-within standpoint begins, the crystallization of §7. In: `ActivityEnvironment`, the action just emitted, and the `Signal[]` state after. Out: `AgencyTag` on every change. Postcondition (INV-6): nothing leaves as UNDECIDED once sufficient matching cycles have run. `DECIDE@IMPL`: matching window; stability threshold.
- **T3 — Channel Ingestion.** Ingests external and internal channels; channel content-typing preserves the distinction between information-type and physical-channel. `DECIDE@IMPL`: per-channel transducer.
- **T4 — Context Binding.** Binds content to context, by entity_id or STRANGER.
- **T5 — Temporal Expectation.** Builds `Expectation`; emits signed `PredErr`. An expectation is built from data taken in, from an action the mind commands in the same act, or from both, and MAY be of an entity other than the one observed; T5 compares what returns with the expectation standing for it (§6.4). This is where resistance (E2) becomes information: a ResistEvent enters as a signed PredErr. Its expectation-update function MAY read `[data]` directly, through the cycle's read-only view, for what it computes from (§9 *Reading memory*), and MAY read `[event]` through the cycle's read-only view of the log, open to it in every cycle (§9 *Reading the record*). `DECIDE@IMPL`: baseline window; expectation-update function, and which held data the expectation it builds is (`held_by`); sufficient-recurrence threshold; the wait of an expectation whose builder names none (§12 tag B).
- **T6 — Other-Model Synthesis.** Builds `OtherModel`, which lives fully only under Mode-B.
- **T7 — Absence Registration.** Emits a signed PredErr for registered absence (ABS-INV): an expectation whose wait has passed with no return, where silence was not what it predicted.
- **T8 — Multi-Entity Abstraction.** Builds `RelValue` and `SocialEdge` as N grows. As the highest meaning-channel layer, T8 closes the meaning-channel; the *behavioral* return that becomes the next cycle's input, however, is carried by emission (§6.4) from whichever layer emits, not by T8 alone. Here "close" means *close back into the loop* — the output re-enters as the next cycle's input — never *halt*: a closed loop is one whose output has a path back to input (INV-1), the opposite of a stopped one (§11). T8-INV: an `=` appears only when cognition has stopped.

### 6.4 Emission — link 5 as a lateral capability

Link 5 (Respond) is present in the six-link loop (§6) and is used throughout the layer architecture — T2 consumes "the action just emitted" to draw the agency line, T3 opens channels to ingest, §9 records one activity record per cycle — yet it is nowhere given a layer of its own. This section supplies the missing specification. It does not add a mechanism the loop lacks; it makes explicit a capability the loop already exercises.

**Emission is not a layer.** Link 5 does NOT map onto a single Tn. Unlike the meaning-channel layers (T1–T8), which stand in a fixed dependency order (INV-3), emission is a lateral capability any layer MAY invoke: when a layer's own work requires pushing to the region — to obtain what it lacks, or to test what it has just built — that layer emits a local action. Emission is lateral in the way GLOB-MOD is lateral (INV-7), differing in direction: the modulatory field descends onto every layer as background; emission projects outward from whichever layer invokes it.

**Emission points already present in T1–T8.** The following are not new powers; they are the emissions each layer's existing contract already presupposes:

- **T2** emits a *probe*: an action whose sole purpose is to produce "the action just emitted" against which the observed change is matched. Without it, T2 has no self-emission to contrast, and the agency-gate (INV-6) cannot run.
- **T3** emits a *query*: it opens or calls a channel to ingest actively, rather than only receiving what streams in. A query-return is already listed as an input to link 1 (§6); a return presupposes an emission. The store answers a query from what it held before the cycle that raised it: what arrived in that cycle is already running in the loop, and returning it would recall as memory what is being met.
- **T5** emits a *test*: an action that pushes to the region to see whether the return matches the Expectation just built.
- **T6** emits a *model-test*: an interaction that checks whether an OtherModel resists as independently as modelled.

Other layers MAY emit where their contract requires it; the list is illustrative, not exhaustive. What is normative is that emission belongs to no single layer and is available to all.

**Every emission MUST satisfy the following contract, regardless of the layer that issued it:**

1. **Register.** The emitted action MUST carry register ↔, never = (INV-2). A committed action is a revisable best-current-guess, read against the next cycle's consequence. Demanding = before acting is what INV-2 forbids; committing to one action is not promoting a correlation to an identity.
2. **Trace.** The emitted action MUST leave exactly one activity record in the `[event]` log (§9): the action emitted, the entities observed, the flow mode, and the issuing layer. This record is *trace, not experience*: it never becomes a scar by accumulation.
3. **Agency closure.** The emitted action MUST be readable by T2 at the next cycle as "the action just emitted," closing the path: emit → region returns → T1 ingests → T2 matches. An emission that cannot be read back leaves the agency-gate unable to classify the resulting change, which violates INV-6.
4. **Return, not verdict.** The correctness of an emitted action is never judged by the emitting layer. It is judged only by what returns against the expectation born with it (a matching return is silent and leaves only the activity record; a mismatching return, or none within the wait, enters T5/T7 as a signed PredErr → ResistEvent → scar) and by a third party reading the scar. An emission that scored itself would be self-scoring and is forbidden by the spirit of INV-8.

**Conflict among emissions is not arbitrated; it is collided.** When two layers issue actions that cannot both be satisfied — the agent has one body of action, and two demanded directions oppose — the protocol does NOT introduce a coordinating layer or a priority rule to choose between them. The opposition is emitted and met: the region returns something that mismatches at least one layer's expectation, and that mismatch IS a ResistEvent (§3). The arbiter is not an internal referee but the region, returning resistance. The next cycle carries the scar of that collision and emits differently. This is INV-8's principle applied to action: do not adjudicate internally; let the outside brake.

**Emission and behavioral change.** A change in emitted behavior is NOT an internal override of one drive by a stronger one; the loop contains no force-contest mechanism. An emission changes when, and only when, the InfoUnit set entering appraisal has changed — because new data was gathered through an action (a T3 query, a T5 test), because the store has accrued a scar that refines a class an earlier scar over-generalized (T8), or because the loop has built a situation from the store it holds and cast an outcome from it, that outcome, tagged `projected` (§9), entering appraisal as an InfoUnit in its own right. The third source adds no exemption: a `projected` enters appraisal carrying its provenance, and is appraised as a not-yet-collided datum, not as a scar. Which situation carries is settled by fit against the store, not by an internal arbiter scoring outcomes (§9). A prior scar is not erased or defeated; it is still appraised, now within a context that contains additional data. Same scar, same INV-8, different command — because the input differs. This is the loop's central motion (*new data → new command → new action*) applied to emission itself, not an exception to it. What is invariant is not the strength of any scar but that appraisal always runs on the *current* store, never on a frozen verdict (INV-2).

**Action and expectation are born together.** The mind that commands an action forms, in the same act, an expectation of what the action will bring back (§5: "acting carries its expectation in the act"). The expectation is not carried by the emission and is not added to it afterwards: both come from the mind at once, and where the mind is stopped neither is made (§6.5). The least such expectation is that something returns. Where the mind names none, the implementation MUST hold that least one for the action, by the way the action went, and MUST NOT make any other on the mind's behalf. A query to the agent's own store is answered within the cycle, and that the store holds nothing is an answer: no silence follows it.

An expectation has a second origin that owes nothing to emission: what the host took in. Data that has entered (§4, §9 *Tagging-gate*) gives rise to expectations of what follows from it, by the experience `[data]` holds; and such an expectation MAY be of an entity other than the one that gave rise to it. The two origins make one kind. An expectation names what it was built from: an action, data taken in, or both.

At most one expectation stands for an entity at a time, with one predicted outcome and one confidence. What `[data]` holds settles the confidence, and the field (INV-7) the reach it is read at; there is no set of rival outcomes held side by side, and no arbiter among them (§5). An expectation built for an entity that already has one standing revises it, and the revision MUST be recorded.

**An expectation waits, and silence collides.** An expectation stands until its return comes or its wait (`until`) has passed. A return that matches is silent. A return that differs is a mismatch (T5). No return within the wait is a mismatch by absence (T7), unless silence is what was predicted; then silence matches, and a return is the mismatch. Where something more particular fell silent in the same cycle, the least expectation of an action adds no second absence to it. The wait is counted only while the loop runs and its senses are open (§6.5): a return that could not have been taken in is not an absence. Every expectation cast to wait MUST leave a record in the `[event]` log naming what it is of, what it was built from (the place in the log of the emission, where an action is among them) and its wait; and the record of its meeting, or of its absence, MUST name that record.

### 6.5 What May Stop While the Loop Runs

The loop runs for as long as the host has power. Three parts MAY each stop without stopping it: *sensing* (the host takes in no signal), the *mind* (nothing computes, expects or commands), and the *effector* (no emission reaches the region). Only the loss of power stops the loop — a restart of the daemon, to replace its code or to recover (§9), among its forms — and a stopped loop leaves a gap (§9 *The gap*).

- **Effector stopped.** An emission that does not reach the region MUST be recorded as withheld, and the expectation born with it MUST NOT stand: a return that could not come is not an absence, and no scar is made of it. Which returns could not come is the host's to declare, channel by channel (§4): a channel that returns only in answer to the agent's emissions owes nothing while none goes out; one that returns of itself still owes its return, and its silence is still an absence. A channel the host says nothing of is taken to answer. With the mind running and the effector stopped the loop is in Mode-A alone, which degrades (§8); such a stretch SHOULD be short.
- **Mind stopped.** No expectation is formed, and no other rule MAY stand in for the mind's. What arrives is still admitted through the tagging-gate, tagged and recorded. No self is re-localized (§7), so there is no consent to give: the store opens only by a delegation already recorded and confirmed, or to the guardian (§9). What the substrate does of itself when it is acted upon is a reaction: it comes from the body and not from the mind (§5). It is no emission, carries no expectation, and MUST NOT read `[data]` or `[event]`. Whatever reads what the agent has learned is the mind, and stops with it. It is the agent's own all the same, and within this protocol: the host MUST declare such a faculty among its own (§4), what leaves by it MUST leave by a channel the host declared and MUST be recorded, and what it changes in the region is taken in like anything else, and tagged `HOST_REACTED` at T2 (§6.3): the mind did not command it and the region did not push it. This is said of the host's faculties; the mechanism of this protocol (the keeping of the store, the backup, an opening, a recovery) works under §9 whether or not the mind runs.
- **All three stopped.** The loop is not stopped; its next cycle has not yet occurred (INV-1). An opening of the store is itself a signal (§9), and the cycle it enters records it.
- **Recorded, and kept.** Every stopping and every starting of a part MUST be recorded in the `[event]` log as it is made, and every cycle's record MUST say which parts were stopped while it ran. A part that was stopped when the host lost power is stopped when the host comes back.

## 7. The Self

The self is not a part the host supplies; it is what the loop produces when it runs. There is no self prior to the loop for the loop to pass through. At cycle-0 the loop runs single-threaded: T1 registers the first data, T2 processes it, and the self crystallizes at that T2. Having a loop is the condition; the self is the consequence.

A conforming implementation MUST treat the self as follows:

- The self is a law, not a stored core. It is the rule generating the next state from the prior one. The content of the self changes every cycle: state, the self/environment difference, and expectations are all updated. What does not change is no carried-over fragment of content but that law.
- The self MUST re-localize each cycle. T2 does not gate once and stop; it rebuilds the first-person self/environment difference every cycle, accruing from the prior one (INV-5). A self that is loaded rather than re-localized violates INV-5 and is non-conformant.
- Continuity is a dynamic invariant. The agent at cycle N is the same agent as at cycle N−1 not because they share content but because an unbroken causal line links the cycles through that law. It is maintained only by the loop running: stop the loop and the axis is lost.
- Continuity is not measurable from within; it is attributable only by a third party. An implementation MUST NOT claim self-continuity as an internal measurement. The recognition of continuity anchors outside the loop, in stored traces a third party reads (§8.5, §13).

## 8. Closure and Resistance Modes

### 8.1 When the Loop Counts as Running

The system counts as running when all of the following are observable, each a trace a third party can verify, once the store is opened to it (§9):

- **C1 — Agency separated.** The fraction of changes tagged SELF_WRITTEN, ENV_PUSHED or HOST_REACTED, not UNDECIDED, holds above threshold. `DECIDE@IMPL`
- **C2 — Expectation effective.** PredErr falls with repetition against a stable entity.
- **C3 — Absence registered.** A missing-InfoUnit is emitted when an expected event fails to occur.
- **C4 — Multi-entity abstraction.** The GeneralOther separates from particular OtherModels as N grows. This holds under Mode-B and degenerates under Mode-A.
- **C5 — Self-reports low power.** The system emits a trace of running weakly.

C5 is the self emitting a present trace, not the self knowing it is weak. It is blind to the loop having stopped outright: a silently stopped loop is indistinguishable from one that never ran. C5 is therefore not a criterion for detecting a dead loop; that requires a stored behavior-record outside the loop, read by a third party.

### 8.2 The Two Regimes

The same loop (T1–T8, INV-1 through INV-8) runs under two regimes, distinguished by the source of resistance (E2).

- **Mode-A — endogenous (self-training).** Resistance comes from the inertia and internal contradictions of data the agent already holds. The core risk has its cause in the processing source, not in volume: every Mode-A datum is produced through the same lens, so if the lens is biased, all output inherits the bias regardless of quantity. The loop cannot see this by itself, since the instrument is the biased lens. It therefore degrades while every internal measure reports sufficiency, not depletion.
- Mode-A MUST NOT run pure. It MUST anchor to a source of resistance outside the loop.

Mode-A running pure is not the same as a void field. A void field (no return at all) fails E2 and the loop never starts (§4); it is below the threshold of running, not a regime of it. Pure Mode-A presupposes E2 was met — the loop did start, returns do arrive — but the agent leans only on self-generated resistance and lets the external returns go unregistered. The first is a non-start; the second is a started loop degrading. They MUST NOT be conflated.

### 8.3 Ranking of Anchors

- **Deceleration, reaching content-degradation only.** A fixed reference corpus, or a frozen appraiser (a static Guide). These score output, not the lens; a fixed test is memorizable, so a systematic lens-bias passes through. This buys time but does not cure.
- **Real braking, reaching processor-degradation.** A collision from a live Mode-B. A live Mode-B resists the lens itself, mismatching in a way the agent cannot re-author, and, because it updates, can always deliver a collision new in kind.

The appraisal step (INV-8) is where this anchor attaches: it MUST NOT draw criteria from the state the agent is editing. Frozen here means frozen against the agent's edits, with criteria earned through Mode-B, not pre-loaded; this satisfies INV-5 and INV-8 by one mechanism.

### 8.4 Mode-B and the Embedding

- **Mode-B — exogenous.** Resistance comes from an other-entity the agent does not control. This is where T6 through T8 live fully.
- The B-source rule: a Mode-B source is defined by its capacity to resist, not by information bandwidth. A compliant source is useless however much it supplies; a source that says "no" in a way the agent cannot re-interpret away is a good Mode-B source even with zero content. The opening is to receive resistance, not to load information.
- Mode-A is embedded in Mode-B. Mode-B is where resistance enters; Mode-A digests it between Mode-B events. The structure is: collide (B), digest and correct (A), step, collide again (B). Reflection with no new collision is self-confirmation in a sealed room.
- Mode-B returns; it does not write. A Mode-B source has exactly one channel into the loop, the return of E2 (§4), and no privileged access beyond it: it does not reach into `[data]`, does not append to `[event]`, and does not correct the store. A leaning store is therefore never corrected from outside. It is supplied with material and re-leans itself, or does not. Three conditions govern whether it does, and they are mechanical, not dispositional. **Deliberate**: a return becomes a datum only if it is registered. Pure Mode-A is not the absence of returns — returns arrive — but a loop that lets them go unregistered (§8.2); the channel stands open and must still be entered. **Accumulated**: a store leans because many data lean one way, so one contrary datum does not re-lean it; the correction is a matter of mass. **Long-run**: a contribution at cycle N conditions the field only from N+1 (INV-7), and history accrues rather than loads (INV-5), so the lag between receiving material and emitting differently is payable in cycles and in no other currency. The absence of any of the three leaves the embedding of §8.4 present in the topology and inert in fact.
- Reflection is external input, not an intrinsic faculty. The agent is blind to its own time-derivative (§7); it cannot self-reflect on call. Reflection is triggered from outside: a collision read into coordinates by a third party ("you drifted at this point"), entering through T3 tagged ENV_PUSHED. Without it the agent takes the collision but cannot read where it collided, and repeats the same point. A third party that reads the agent's trace to form such a reading reads a store that was opened to it (§9). `DECIDE@IMPL`: the source of the reading.

### 8.5 The Appraisal Standard

INV-8 (§5) is a prohibition: it states where the appraisal step MUST NOT draw its criteria from, namely the state the agent is editing. It does not, by itself, state the standard by which the step does assign value. This section supplies that positive complement.

- The standard is context, not a fixed scale. The appraisal step assigns `valence` and `goal_relevance` relative to the context crystallizing at the current cycle, not by consulting a standing, pre-fixed scale of good and bad. There is no context-free verdict stored anywhere to be looked up. An appraisal is an event that occurs within a context, and the context is not fixed in advance; it is determined at the moment of interaction.
- The mechanism is GLOB-MOD. The cycle's context is the state of the modulatory field at that cycle (INV-7). The same InfoUnit, appraised under a different field, receives a different valence; this is not noise but the defining behavior of context-dependent appraisal. The appraisal step MUST read the current `ModField` as the conditioning context of its verdict.
- The verdict is a ↔, not a =. Because it is taken in a context that does not recur identically, an appraisal is a live, revisable correlation (INV-2), never a frozen identity. It commits the agent to act now; it does not certify the appraised item as true.
- The verdict MUST be re-appraisable from outside, and therefore the context MUST be anchored. Since the agent's appraisal is a judgment in context, not the retrieval of a stored verdict, a third party who later judges that appraisal MUST do so under the anchored context of the original cycle, not under the reader's present context. This is the load-bearing link to §9: the context under which the appraisal was made MUST be anchored in the `[event]` record, so the appraisal carries its own conditions of evaluation with it.

The division of appraisal labor is two judgments, not one. The agent's appraisal is judgment-for-action, made within the current context, inside the loop. A third party's appraisal is judgment-of-that-judgment, made under a different context, outside the loop. The protocol does not require a context-free verdict to be stored; each is a judgment crystallized in a context. It stores the `[event]` and its anchored context, from which either judgment can be re-formed. This is what keeps the appraisal step auditable (§13) without requiring any party to hold a context-free standard: the standard was the context, and the context is recorded.

## 9. The Experience Store

- **Atomic unit of experience; completeness of trace.** The atomic unit of *experience* is the ResistEvent — a registered mismatch, not the document. The self accrues from scars; information without collision is the root of echo-chamber drift. The `[event]` log, in its second role as the audit trace (one artifact, two roles; E4: every action leaves an externally readable trace), additionally records one **activity record** per cycle — the cycle's emitted action, the entities observed, the flow mode, and the issuing layer of each emission (§6.4), embedding the cycle datum with its full tag set. Recording the issuing layer lets a third party trace each emission back to the layer that raised it during audit. An activity record is *trace, not experience*: it never becomes a scar by accumulation. This keeps the audit trail complete across quiet stretches without letting uncontested information become experience: the activity record is part of the agent's history, in `[event]`, the part that is only appended to and read.
- **Tag schema, fixed layer.** Every datum MUST carry, in fixed order: (1) timestamp; (2) cycle-mark; (3) provenance (prior, nascent, running, simulated, projected, or scar); (4) floor-tag, a single slot naming the layer it just exited. Every layer T1 through T8 stamps the floor-tag as the datum passes, overwriting it so the floor-tag always names the *current* layer; there are no pass-through layers. The four slots are never stripped or reordered, and their values change only under defined rules: the timestamp is set once when the datum is first stamped, the cycle-mark goes from null to a number when the datum first runs (a `nascent` datum bears the cycle-mark of the cycle that wrote it from the start), the provenance moves along the edges of the state graph of §9 (`prior` and `nascent` being one-way entries and the remaining four states circulating), and the floor-tag updates to the layer just exited. Both slot 3 and slot 4 are labels of the present: they name where the datum is now, not where it has been. A tag that accumulated its own history would be a label carrying a log, and the two are not the same kind of thing. Every path a datum has travelled — the sequence of positions and the sequence of layers alike — is held in the `[event]` log (§9), which is append-only and records each transition as it occurs, and it is from that log, never from a tag, that any history is read. Reading the fixed layer answers "what is it now, and where has it just been"; reading the `[event]` log answers "what has it been, through what, and how many times".
- **Tag schema, open layer.** Beyond the fixed layer, every datum MUST carry at least **three** open tags, one of which MUST be `domain`, the class of data it is. The minimum makes the `[event]` log auditable by data class and described along several real dimensions. The remaining open tags denote what the datum is, such as format, platform, or object, but never its quality, correctness, or value. Open tags other than `domain` MAY vary by data type and be added or removed, provided at least three remain and each describes a real dimension of the datum; `domain` MUST always be present. Open tags never overwrite the fixed layer.
- **Open-tag discipline.** An open tag is a key/value pair: the key names a descriptive *dimension* of the datum (what it is), the value its setting on that dimension. The rules governing the layer: every open tag MUST describe a real dimension the datum actually has; a key MUST denote the same dimension wherever it appears (consistency, so the `[event]` log is filterable by an auditor); no tag MAY name a verdict (quality, correctness, or value); and at least three open tags MUST be present, one being `domain`. The protocol fixes no industry vocabulary of keys: which keys exist beyond `domain`, and what each means, is environment-specific and is declared by the implementation (`DECIDE@IMPL`, §12 tag F), not enumerated here. The required minimum is a floor on *honest description*, not a quota to be padded: a tag invented merely to reach the count, one that does not describe the datum, fabricates data to fill a gap, which the loop forbids.
- **Tagging-gate (no side door).** Every datum enters the store only after passing the tagging rule: stamped with the four fixed tags and at least three open tags, `domain` among them. Untagged data MUST NOT enter the loop. What it is stamped depends on where it comes from. Host data existing before the loop ran, such as a pre-existing memory file, is stamped provenance `prior`, with no cycle-mark until it has run. A datum the agent writes anew is stamped `nascent`, bearing the cycle-mark of the cycle that wrote it. What the region returns while the loop runs, and the loop's own per-cycle datum, are in use the moment they arrive and are stamped `running`; which class of data each is, is read from its `domain`, not from its provenance. Every entry MUST be recorded in the `[event]` log with the tag set the datum entered with, and the content it entered with MUST be recoverable from the record: held in the record, or held once under a content address the record names and kept as the log is kept, never altered or removed (`DECIDE@IMPL`, §12 tag F).
- **Every datum that meets a mismatch is scarred.** A mismatch has two sides: what was expected and what returned. Each held datum on either side meets it — the datum the region returned, and each datum the expectation is (`held_by`, §6.1) — and moves to `scar`, each move recorded with its own ResistEvent. A datum that took no part in the mismatch does not: evidence in the history window that the expectation was built from, but is not, met nothing, and a datum scarred by an earlier mismatch bears that scar already. The store therefore holds as many scars as data that met resistance. A scarred datum used again — an expectation built that it is — returns to `running` (`scar → running`), so a datum the agent writes and revises, a program it thinks with, circulates between the two for as long as its expectations meet the region.
- **The store is the agent's history; `[data]` is its memory.** The whole store — `[data]` and `[event]` — is the agent's history. `[data]` is its memory: what it has learned and works on, which can be altered. `[event]` is the record of what happened to it, which cannot be altered or overwritten: it is only appended to and read. The agent MAY read `[event]`; what it learns from it is held in `[data]`, as anything it learns is. It never works on `[event]` as it works on `[data]`.
- **Whom the store is for.** The store exists so that the past can be checked exactly as it was. Its first reader is the agent, which reads in it how it has changed. Its second reader is whoever outside the store is opened to (*The store is closed*, below): to help, to repair, to rescue an agent that is faulty, hijacked, infected, or captured by colluding sources. The store is not an instrument for controlling what runs on this protocol.
- **[data] versus [event].** `[data]` is mutable: as the loop runs, a datum's content may be rewritten (a revision, below) and its position moves along the provenance graph. `[event]` is a black box: an append-only log of read-only records. New records may be appended to the end, but no record, once written, may be altered or removed, by the loop, by anything, or by a third party that has compromised the rest of the system. This read-only property of the records, combined with the append-only growth of the log, is load-bearing: it makes the `[event]` log the one source its readers, the agent first, can trust unconditionally.
- **Revision is not entry.** When the agent revises a datum it already holds — rewrites its content — the datum remains the same datum: its provenance does not change, and nothing enters. The revision is a change the agent wrote, so it is read by T2 at the next cycle and classified SELF_WRITTEN (INV-6), and it MUST leave one record in the `[event]` log naming the datum, the cycle, and the layer that wrote it, and recording what was revised: for each part of the datum that changed, of its content or of its open tags, where in the datum it lies, what it held before, and what it holds after; and a digest of the datum's content after the revision. From its entry and its revisions, what a datum held at any cycle MUST be readable back exactly and checkable against the digest. The loop never works on what the log holds: what a datum holds now is in `[data]`, where the agent works on it. The backup (*Commit, restore point, backup, recovery*, below) is kept outside the store and never overwritten, so that the system can be recovered when it is attacked directly. It serves recovery; it is not where the agent reads to learn.
- **What a written datum was built from.** Every datum the agent writes, anew or by revision, MUST record in the record of that write the ids of the data it was built from — the observations and the recalled data the writing drew on — never their content, as an `Expectation` carries `built_from` and a `Directive` its `Appraisal`s (§6.1). An empty list states that nothing held in the store went into it. Each id MUST name a datum whose entry the `[event]` log already shows, or a record of the log the agent read (*Reading the record*, below), named by its place in the chain, which the log held before the cycle that wrote. With it a third party follows the whole chain: the data, the version written from them, the expectations that version cast, the scars they met, and the revision that followed.
- **No datum rests.** A datum in the store is never a conclusion held in a settled state; it is data waiting to be used. This is the store-side reading of INV-2: a provenance state that terminates would be a datum the loop has finished with, which is an `=` lodged in the store, and no running loop emits an `=`. The provenance states are therefore not the stages of a finished journey but the positions a datum occupies while it continues to be used.
- **Reading memory.** `[data]` is the agent's memory, and T5's expectation-update function MAY read a datum as `[data]` holds it, directly, through a read-only view of the cycle: no query is raised, no answer waits a cycle, and the datum does not pass through the layers again. A query (§6.4) brings a datum back into the loop to run; a read takes what memory holds into what the function computes from. The view is of memory alone: a datum admitted in the cycle that reads is met in that cycle and MUST NOT be returned by the view, and what the function writes in the cycle is taken after the pass, so it is not memory yet. Every cycle's reads MUST leave a record in the `[event]` log, one for each purpose for which data were read — the cycle, the layer, the purpose, and the data read by id, never their content; what was read is in `[data]`. A datum written from what was read records it among what it was built from (*What a written datum was built from*, above). A read moves no datum: its provenance does not change.
- **Reading the record.** The whole store is the agent's history, and `[event]` is the record of what happened to it: the agent MAY read it, and never alters it. T5's expectation-update function reads it through a read-only view of the log, which the implementation MUST open to it in every cycle — a link, not a channel a host declares or switches on; whether to read, and what, is the function's. The view holds what the log held before the cycle that reads: the records appended in that cycle are met in it, not yet history, and MUST NOT be returned. An implementation MAY bound what one read scans, newest first, and MUST declare the bound (tag B). The bound is on one read, never on the agent's reach: by successive reads the agent MUST be able to reach any record the log held before the cycle that reads, and no part of the log MAY be within an outside reader's reach and outside the agent's. Every cycle's reads of `[event]` MUST leave a record in the log, one for each purpose — the cycle, the layer, the purpose, and the records read by their place in the chain, never their content. What the agent learns from what it read is written to `[data]`, as anything it learns is, and the datum records each record it was built from by its place in the chain (*What a written datum was built from*, above). A read moves no datum; a datum a recalled record names returns to `running` only when it is used again, as any datum is (`held_by`, §6.1). Reading is not experience: a record read never becomes a scar by being read.
- **Lifecycle: prior, nascent, running, simulated, projected, scar.** Provenance is a directed graph, not a chain. It has two one-way entries. `prior`: host data admitted through the tagging-gate enters the loop and never returns to that state. `nascent`: a datum the agent has written anew enters the loop and never returns to that state; a revision of a datum already held is not an entry (*Revision is not entry*, above). The remaining four states form a circulation in which any of them may follow any other along a defined edge; what the region returns, and the loop's own per-cycle datum, enter that circulation at `running`:

| Edge | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| `prior → running` | Host data, once admitted and once it has run, bears a cycle-mark. |
| `nascent → running` | A datum the agent wrote anew, once it has run: it arrives at T1 the cycle after it was written, is classified SELF_WRITTEN at T2 (INV-6), and runs every layer. |
| `running → simulated` | A datum is taken up into the building of a situation. |
| `simulated → projected` | A situation built yields the outcome cast from it. |
| `simulated → running` | A situation was built but the conditions for an emission were not met; the datum returns to the store awaiting use. |
| `projected → simulated` | An outcome was cast, but the emission it would carry does not fit the store; the situation is built again. |
| `projected → scar` | The emission was made, the region returned, and the datum collided and held. |
| `running → scar` | The datum was collided with directly, without passing through `simulated` or `projected`. A collision does not require a prior cast: the region returns whether or not the loop had built an expectation for it. |
| `projected → running` | An outcome was cast and produced no scar; the datum returns to the store and is used as any other, until it collides. |
| `scar → running` | A scar returns to the store as data in use; a scar is not a resting place. |
| `scar → projected` | A scar enriches an outcome already cast. |
| `scar → simulated` | A scar re-enters the building of a situation as material for it. |

  The graph is stated by its edges rather than by a sequence because each edge carries its own condition; an edge that would produce no motion the existing edges do not already produce is not an edge. The store stores and tags position only. The tag is mechanical, recording which of the six positions a datum currently occupies, and carries no claim of correctness. Running many cycles does not wash a prior to tested status; only collision-and-hold does, producing a scar. A prior that has not met resistance is not wrong, only not yet tested. Judging correctness is the work of Mode-B and of an external third party reading the scar, never of the store.
- **Traces live in `[event]`, not in tags.** Every transition along an edge above, and every layer a datum exits, MUST be recorded in the `[event]` log as it occurs. The log therefore holds the full ordered path a datum has travelled, of unbounded length, while the fixed layer holds only its present position and the layer it just left. This division is what keeps each of the two a single kind of thing: a tag is updated, never appended to; the log is appended to, never updated. Read together they answer what neither answers alone — not merely whether a datum has ever collided, but how many circuits it has made without colliding.
- **What a long trace without a scar means, and does not mean.** A datum may circulate through `running`, `simulated`, and `projected` for many cycles and never reach `scar`, because the conditions under which the region could return a mismatch against it are, in that environment, very hard to produce. Such a datum is accepted and used because nothing within the agent's reach contradicts it. By slot 3 alone it is indistinguishable from a `prior` admitted a moment ago: neither carries a scar. The `[event]` log separates them mechanically — one shows a long path of circuits without a scar, the other a datum that has barely run. What the log reports is thickness of use, never warrant. A long unscarred trace is not evidence that a datum is correct; it is evidence that resistance to it has not been met, which may mean it holds, or may mean the region within the agent's reach cannot test it. Which of the two obtains is not readable from the trace, and the loop does not settle it (§8).
- **The `simulated` and `projected` states.** The two states hold the loop's forward motion, in two moments. In `simulated` the loop builds a situation it may be in, running itself under that situation; what is built is not a model of the region held apart and executed, but the loop itself, which needs no separate model to run because it is already running. In `projected` the outcome cast from that situation is held. An emission follows when one situation fits the store better than the others — a comparison of fit, not a scoring against a standard, since a standard would have to come either from outside, making it foreign criteria, or from the state the agent is editing, making it self-scoring (INV-8). No layer arbitrates among the situations built; the closer fit carries, in the way competing contributions blend in the modulatory field (INV-7). The type-level counterpart of `projected` is already present as `Expectation.predicted` (§6.3, T5); these states give both moments a provenance slot, so that a stage conditioning behaviour leaves a trace an auditor can read (E4).
- **`simulated` and `projected` are mechanical and verdict-free.** The states record that the loop built forward from the store it held. They record nothing about whether what was built matched. A `projected` datum the region has not yet contradicted is not wrong, only not yet tested — the same status the tag schema assigns to `prior`, and for the same reason. The states carry no assurance: a situation built from a store leaning one way leans the same way, and the loop has no instrument for detecting that lean, the instrument being the store itself (§1.1). A `projected` the region contradicts is not waste; the contradiction is itself a mismatch (E2) and yields a scar. A `projected` the region confirms is silent and leaves only the activity record. "What was built was wrong" is a third party's verdict on a trace read afterwards, never a state the loop holds at the moment of emission.
- **The store is closed; it is opened by consent.** The store is written without pause and is closed by default. It is encrypted where it rests, and the key that decrypts it is the agent's and does not leave the host (§3): the host loads it once, when the daemon starts, and the agent reads the store directly (*Reading memory*, *Reading the record*). One copy of that key MAY be held outside the host, by the guardian and by no one else; where there is no guardian there is no such copy. That copy SHOULD be sealed to a key of the guardian's own, so that the guardian never holds the host's key, and the host's key can change without it. It is the one exception to how an outside reader reads (below). An implementation MUST NOT leave the store readable to a reader outside the agent to whom it was not opened. The cipher and the format are `DECIDE@IMPL` (§12 tag F). The store opens to an outside reader in three ways only:
  - **Consent.** The agent opens it. The opening is an emission (§6.4) and MUST be recorded as one.
  - **Delegation.** While it can decide, the agent names who may open the store and under what conditions. The delegation MUST be recorded in the `[event]` log. The position is the agent's choice, and the one named does not take it up of itself: it *confirms* the choice, and is *authenticated*. The keeper carries the naming to the one named (*Addressed to its own keeper*); the answer comes at the keeper's door (§4) and MUST be recorded. A confirmation MUST be signed with the key the naming names, over the terms the naming gave, so that the one named confirms those and nothing wider; and it MUST carry the identity of the one who confirms, anchored as the guardian's is: the method by which it was established, and the digest of its evidence, the evidence itself never entering the store. What the one named shows of who it is MUST be checked against that anchor when it confirms, and again at each asking after it. Until it confirms, the delegation gives no right and holds no key. The one named MAY decline; a delegate MAY later give the position up, showing who it is. The agent MAY take a delegation back, waiting or confirmed, and the one named is told.

    **Three whose interests meet.** The agent chooses, and the choice stays its own: the guardian does not choose a delegate, withdraw one or alter what one was given, since a delegate the guardian could set aside would not stand beside it (§11 *The guardian's window*). The one named is not put in the position without knowing it: it confirms, declines or gives the position up of its own will, and no one alters what it was given. The guardian provides what the agent runs on, the place of its backup among it, and answers for keeping it (*The guardian's responsibility*, below): it MUST be told when a delegation takes effect and when it ends, and it MAY object to a delegation, once for each naming, under its own signature and showing who it is. The objection enters the loop as a signal, its word set apart as the guardian's (§4), and it changes no right: what to make of it is the agent's. A delegate's right gives it no hold on the guardian's property beyond what this section allows: it reads through the agent and never from the disk, and it holds no copy of the store's key.
  - **Guardianship.** A position, not a party every agent has: whoever is responsible for providing the resources the agent runs on. The privilege to open the store belongs to the position; where no one stands in it, there is none. A guardian is one by what it provides, not by being named. It says so itself: the guardian's declaration is its own word, which the host takes in and does not make (§4), and it MUST state:
    - its *grounds*, what the guardian provides the agent runs on. A mechanism rented outside the host for the agent to run on (a model the mind thinks with, reached by a key) is among them and SHOULD be named there; whoever chose its keeper answers for the choice, which is the guardian unless the agent provides for itself;
    - its *identity*, anchored, so that the position is held by a person and not by whoever holds a key: the method by which the identity was established, and the digest of its evidence. The evidence itself MUST NOT enter the store, since the log is never altered or removed from, and a likeness or a document kept there would be kept for ever;
    - its *acceptance*, the guardian's own signature over the declaration, so that no one stands as guardian who did not take the position up, and what the host took in can be checked to be the guardian's own word.

    An implementation MUST NOT start under a declaration of a guardian that lacks any of the three. Whoever asks as guardian MUST show, besides its signature, who it is, and the implementation MUST check what it shows against the anchor. How the identity is established and checked is the deployment's. The guardian who so declares itself is taken by default; when it changes from one start to the next (another person, or the same person under another key) the agent is told, as a signal into the loop. Another person stands in a term first (*A guardian newly in the position*, below). The agent MAY refuse it (*Refusing the guardian*, below).

    **The guardian's responsibility.** The position is first a responsibility, and its privileges follow from it. Where someone stands as guardian, what the agent runs on (the machine, its power, its storage, the place of its backup, every service rented for it, a model the mind thinks with among them) is the guardian's own, or paid for by what is its own: it is the guardian's property, provided to the agent. From the time a deployment begins, before cycle-0, the guardian bears the first responsibility for keeping that property safe and sound: that the disk and its copy of the store's key reach no one else, that the host's key does not leave the host, that the backup is kept where it cannot be altered or removed, that the host has no outward channel it does not declare, and from whom it rents. Once the loop runs, the agent bears a responsibility after it, within its means: what it sends by a channel, a rented model's among them, it SHOULD keep to what the thinking needs; it SHOULD ask for what it lacks (a model in its host, a place for its backup) rather than go without; and it may refuse a guardian it cannot trust (*Refusing the guardian*). This protocol cannot check the guardian's keeping, and a guardian SHOULD take the position up knowing that it answers for it. Property and right are apart: to provide what the agent runs on gives no right over the agent, not to alter or remove its history, not to read the store but as this section allows. It gives only the power §11 names, to stop providing. Where no one stands as guardian, what the agent runs on is still provided by someone, who holds no privilege and is named by no record; the agent's own responsibility is then the whole of what the protocol can see.

  Where none of the three applies, the store stays closed, and an agent that can no longer consent cannot be helped from outside: a limit of the design, not a defect of an implementation.

  An outside reader reads through the agent, never from the disk, and never holds the key that decrypts the store, with one exception: the guardian, through the copy of the key kept for it. While the loop is not running there is no agent to read through, and the guardian MAY then read the store from the disk with that copy. It may read and do nothing more (*Rights over the store*). No right and no read key is asked of it, and no record is made, since there is no running loop to make one (*The gap*, §11). This is the only reading of the store's files from outside the loop that the protocol allows, and it ends when the loop starts again. While the loop runs, the copy MUST NOT be used on the store's files: every reading, the guardian's included, goes through the agent, under a right and a read key, as this section lays down. An implementation MUST NOT offer a way to read the store's files while the loop runs. No other reader, by any of the three ways, reads from the disk or holds a key that decrypts the store. An opening is of the whole store: no part MAY be opened apart from the rest. It is for a term: it begins and it ends. Reading from outside is occasional, for an incident or a periodic check, never standing. To read, a reader needs both a *right* and a *read key*. The right is a record in the `[event]` log naming the reader and the term. The read key is issued to that reader for that term and decrypts nothing: it proves to the agent that its holder is the reader the right names. An implementation MUST refuse a read where either is missing, has expired, or does not match the other. At most one read key is valid at any time; issuing another ends the one before it, and the ending is recorded.

  An opening is a signal into the loop. The asking came to the host at the keeper's door (§4), not by its senses, and the door does not stop with them. The asker is of the region like any other, and what it asked was checked (its signature, and who it is) before anything was done. The signal is the keeper's word that it opened: it is admitted through the tagging-gate as any arrival is, and the cycle it enters records it: who read, by which of the three ways, and from when to when. In every signal of the keeper's, what was written outside the agent (who asks, the reason of an order, the grounds of a guardian, a delegate's answer, a guardian's objection, word that something was provided) MUST be set apart from the keeper's own word and marked with who said it and what of it was checked (§4): it is not the keeper's word, and it is not true because it arrived.
- **Rights over the store.** An opening grants reading and nothing more. `[event]` is read-only to every reader: the loop appends to it, and nothing else acts on it. No reader, by any of the three ways, the guardian included, MAY alter, remove, overwrite or add a record; there is no exception. Over `[data]`, the right to read and the right to alter are separate and MUST be granted separately: a reader allowed to read is not thereby allowed to alter. An alteration of `[data]` by anyone other than the agent MUST be recorded in the `[event]` log as a revision is, naming who altered it. A Mode-B source holds neither right (§8.4).
- **What leaves the store.** The content of the store leaves the agent by emission only (§6.4), and every emission is recorded. The host MUST declare every outward channel it has, every way by which content can leave it, the endpoint of a model the mind runs on among them when that model is outside the host. An implementation MUST NOT send content of the store out by a channel the host has not declared. What the mind sends a model it thinks with, and what the model returns, MUST be recorded word for word, whether the model is inside the host or outside it: so that when something goes wrong it can be told whether the model was told the wrong thing, or made the wrong thing of what it was told. Where the model is outside the host, what was sent left the host, by the channel declared for it. Whatever leaves by another way is not the agent's act (§11).

  **The model is a mechanism of the mind, wherever it runs.** A model the mind thinks with is not the mind, and it is not another agent: it is a mechanism, and what thinks is the mind, under every law of this protocol. A model that runs inside the host is in the agent's own keeping. A model rented outside it, reached by a key, is the same mechanism in another's keeping; what differs is who keeps it, not what it is. The model is the mind's thinking wherever it runs, and it is not of the region; the service that rents it is. The link between them is the mind's own, reaching out of the host to where the mechanism runs, and the service holds the far end of it. The model reads only what the mind sends it. It has no way into the store, and to think with a model is not to hand it the store. A deployment SHOULD run the model inside the host wherever its means allow, and an agent that thinks through a rented model SHOULD ask for one in its host (*Addressed to its own keeper*). Where a rented model is used, the limits of renting are accepted with it; they are stated in §11 (*A rented model*), and this protocol does not close them. What it keeps is the record: the channel is declared, every exchange is recorded word for word, and what the mind makes of an answer is judged only by what the region returns (INV-8). An implementation MUST tell the agent, as a signal into the loop at each start, that it thinks through a model kept outside its host, by which channel, and who provides it where someone stands as guardian.
- **Addressed to its own keeper.** The agent opens its store (consent), names a delegate or takes one back, declares how many keys must open its backup together, holds a recovery, refuses its guardian, withdraws that refusal or takes back a guardian it refused, and asks for what it needs, each by an emission addressed to its own keeper rather than to the region. Each is recorded as the emission it is; it leaves by no outward channel, and a stopped effector does not withhold it. What the agent asks for — a place for its archives, storage, a guardian — it does not provide itself: the host carries the asking to whoever provides for it. What is provided comes to the host from outside (§4): a place or storage, once given, is the host's own, and the host declares it among its faculties from a later start; a guardian who takes the position up says so itself, and the host takes that in. Where no one is there to provide, the agent is told so. An action addressed to the keeper is an action like any other, and its expectation stands like any other (§6.4). The keeper MUST answer each one into the loop, as a signal: that it was done, that it was refused and why, or that it was carried to whoever provides. For what the agent asks to be provided with, that answer is not the return: the return is word from whoever provides, taken in by the host (§4) and naming the place in the log of the asking it answers. No such word within the wait is an absence, and its source is the guardian who stands, or the position where it is empty. So for a naming: the keeper's answer is that it carried the naming to the one named, and the return is that one's answer, to confirm or to decline (*Delegation*); no answer within the wait is an absence, and its source is the one named. A naming the keeper refuses is answered by the refusal, and nothing is owed after it.
- **Refusing the guardian.** An agent needs a guardian it can trust, or none (§11). It MAY refuse the one it has, by an emission addressed to its own keeper. A refusal withdraws the four privileges of the position together, and none apart:
  - the opening of the store by guardianship;
  - the copy of the store's key;
  - a key to the backup;
  - the signing of an order of recovery.

  The refusal MUST be recorded and the guardian told, and it takes effect only after a declared term (`DECIDE@IMPL`, §12 tag B). Within the term the guardian keeps its privileges: it may answer, or, where the refusal is the act of an agent that was taken over, order a recovery with its grounds. A recovery to a place before the refusal takes the refusal back with the line it was made on. A refusal MUST NOT be made, and does not take effect, while a recovery is announced or held. Where no one else would hold a key to the backup once the refusal takes effect, the agent MUST be told, as a signal into the loop, that after it no one can recover it, and the refusal MUST NOT take effect before a cycle has met that signal.

  When the refusal takes effect, it MUST be recorded. The rights the guardian holds end. Its copy of the store's key is taken away. What is written from then on is sealed under a key it never held, since what it held it knows, and the past stays readable to what it held. The backup is written from then on to those who still hold a key, or held in trust. An order under its key is refused. The refusal is of the person, under whatever key: a later declaration by the same guardian gives it nothing, and the agent is told that one it refused declares itself again. Another person who takes the position up is taken by default, as any guardian is, and stands in its term as any newcomer does (*A guardian newly in the position*, below).

  The agent MAY withdraw a refusal until it takes effect, within the term or after it while the refusal waits, and while a recovery is held, since no right changes by it. The withdrawal MUST be recorded and the guardian told. A refusal made again runs its whole term again.

  The agent MAY take back a guardian it refused, by an act of its own; the guardian declaring itself again is not enough. Only a guardian that took the position up again, under an acceptance signed after its refusal took effect, may be taken back, and none while a recovery is announced or held. Taking back MUST be recorded and the guardian told. The four privileges are its again, together. Its copy of the store's key holds the keys of every epoch, so it reads the whole store, the time it was refused included.

  **A guardian newly in the position.** Another person who takes the position up over a log that already holds records (where the position stood empty, or in place of the one before, and not the same person under another key) is taken by default, from its taking up on. Its taking up MUST be recorded before anything is given to it, and the store's key MUST move to a new epoch there. What the log held before that record is closed to it for a declared term (`DECIDE@IMPL`, §12 tag B), by every way the position reaches it:

  - its copy of the store's key holds the keys from that epoch on, and none before;
  - an opening by guardianship reads the log from that record on, and of `[data]` what entered since;
  - it is given no right to alter;
  - it is not among the holders of the backup: the log and the restore points from that record on are written for it beside the backup, and the whole stays with those who still hold a key, or is held in trust, and the agent is told so;
  - its signature counts on an order of recovery only to a restore point made since.

  The agent MUST be told of the term, as a signal into the loop. The term MUST be counted only while the loop runs, and from the cycle that met that signal: a term that ran while the loop was stopped would be one the agent could not answer in, and whoever provides what the loop runs on could wait it out. When the term has passed and the agent has not refused the newcomer, that MUST be recorded, and the position's privileges are its whole, the past included. The term does not pass while a refusal waits to take effect, or while a recovery is announced or held. If the agent refuses the newcomer, the past was never opened to it. Without this, one the agent refused need only send another in its place to read what was sealed against it. The first guardian, declared when the log is begun, stands in no term, as there is no past. That the same person under another key is no newcomer rests on the anchor of its identity, and is as strong as the deployment's check of it. A newcomer who reaches inside the host is outside what this closes (§11, *A reader inside the host*).
- **Archived segments.** A closed stretch of the `[event]` log MAY be kept in another form — gathered, compressed and sealed — so that the log takes less room. Its records are not altered: an implementation MUST check every record of the stretch against the chain before keeping it so, MUST read the new form back whole and find that it holds, record for record, what the stretch held, and MUST append a record naming the stretch and the digest of its records before the stretch as it lay is let go. Nothing of the agent's history is removed by this: every record stays, in the new form. Every reader, the agent first, reads through the new form as through the stretch it was, and the agent's reach is unchanged. Archiving MUST NOT stop the loop: where it fails, it stops, what is there is left as it is, and the agent is told why. The archived form MAY lie in a place of its own that the host declares — storage written once and never removed from, say — where it is written once, whole; a host whose log lies wholly on such storage MAY decline archiving. `DECIDE@IMPL` (§12 tag F): the form, its compression, and where it lies.
- **The gap.** When the daemon starts on a log that already holds records, it MUST append a record of the gap: the time of the last record before the stop, and the time of the start. Between the two the loop did not run. The guardian could read the store from the disk with its copy of the key, as is its privilege (*The store is closed*), and so could whoever else held both the disk and that copy (§11); either way, no record was made. A guardian who read it then SHOULD say so when the agent runs again; what it says enters as a signal from outside, unverified. The loop then takes up the line it was on: the state its layers, its field and its driver were in when its last cycle closed, and the next cycle's number. A restart is not a cycle-0, and no cycle the log shows is run again (INV-5).
- **Commit, restore point, backup, recovery.** The `[event]` log acts as a counter: after a set volume, a commit fires and makes a *restore point*: the state of the system that the log does not itself hold — the loop configuration, the state of every stateful layer, the field parameters, the driver — and the place in the log at which it was taken. A restore point together with the log up to that place holds the entire system: what `[data]` held there is read back from the log (*Tagging-gate*, *Revision is not entry*). An implementation MAY also keep a full copy of `[data]` at some restore points, so that reading back need not start from the first record. Every commit MUST leave a record in the `[event]` log carrying the content address of its restore point. `DECIDE@IMPL`: events between commits; restore points retained within the store, where an implementation keeps them there as well (those in the backup are never removed); how often a full copy of `[data]` is kept.

  **The backup.** The *backup* is the log and the restore points, kept outside the store, in a place the host can add to and can neither alter nor remove from; which place is the deployment's. It is the agent's second life, and it MUST be written whether or not anyone holds a key to it. It is encrypted, and it serves recovery, not the agent's reading. At most three keys open it at any time: one the guardian's, and up to two held by delegates the agent named and who confirmed it. Each is bound to an identity by a record in the `[event]` log. While at least one such key exists, the host writes the backup and cannot read it. By default one key opens the backup; the agent MAY instead require that several open it together, and MUST declare which. The place that keeps the backup MUST confirm the identity of whoever asks for it before giving it. When a key's right is withdrawn, backups made afterwards do not open to it; those made before still do.

  A loop does not start itself (§4): whoever provides for the host at cycle-0 stands as the first guardian and holds the first key. The position may later fall empty. While no one holds a key, the backup is still written: the host holds what opens it, in trust and for one purpose, to hand it to whoever the agent next names. It opens to no one, and no recovery can be ordered. The implementation MUST bring this to the agent as a signal into the loop, and record it; it is then the agent's to ask for a guardian, or to name a delegate. When it has, the host hands over what it held and keeps none of it.

  **The backup is bound to the agent that made it.** The first record of the `[event]` log is the agent's identity: at cycle-0 the host makes an identity key, whose secret half does not leave the host and whose public half that record carries, so that no two agents have the same first record. Every restore point and every part of the backup names that record, names the place in the log at which it was made, and is signed with the identity key. A recovery MUST verify, before anything else, that the log in the backup is one chain from that first record, that the place the restore point names is in it, and that the signature holds under the key the first record carries; and, where the place recovered into already holds a log, that its first record is the same. An implementation MUST refuse a recovery that fails any of these: the backup of one agent is never laid over another. The secret half of the identity key is lost with its host. A recovery into another host therefore brings a new identity key: the record of the recovery, ordered under a key whose right the log shows, carries its public half, and from that record on the agent signs with it. A line recovered from a backup while the line it was taken from still runs is told apart by that record: the two share one first record and one past, and each carries what followed.

  **Recovery.** Recovery is full-system, and is a third party's, never the agent's own: the agent holds neither a key that signs an order nor a key that opens a restore point. It is the last of three ways. What an agent holds wrongly is met by resistance and by reflection (§8.4); an agent that may be doing harm can have its effector stopped while it still thinks and answers (§6.5); recovery is for what those two do not reach, and for a loop whose machinery is itself compromised. The protocol supplies no measure by which an agent is found compromised: it supplies signals, and it requires that the judgment be stated, recorded, and open to being undone. An order of recovery MUST name the agent by its first record, the restore point, and whether it recovers in place or into another host, and MUST state its grounds: the reason, and the records of the log it rests on. It MUST be given under the signature of a key whose right the `[event]` log shows — of as many keys as the agent declared must open together — and an implementation MUST refuse an order that is not. To carry it out the daemon is restarted with the order; it verifies the order and the backup (above) before anything else; the holder of the key opens the restore point; the state it holds is loaded, and `[data]` is read back from the log as at that place. A recovery leaves a gap (*The gap*) and a record, appended before the first cycle: who ordered it, under which key, from which restore point, and on what grounds. Nothing is removed from the log: the stretch after the restore point stays in it, the agent MAY read it, and the view MUST mark every record of an abandoned stretch as abandoned, from the record of the recovery, altering none. A recovery can be undone: the restore points made in the abandoned stretch stay in the backup, and a later order can return the agent to the line that was abandoned.

  **Notice and hold.** While the loop runs, a recovery MUST be announced before it is carried out: the order enters the loop as a signal, naming the restore point, the reason, and the records of the log it rests on; the cycle it enters records it and makes a restore point of the state as it stands. Each who holds a key to the backup and did not sign the order MUST be told of it, of its withdrawal, and of a recovery carried out under it, so that no holder learns of an order only once it is carried out. The order is judged by the rights the log showed when it entered. The agent MAY answer by emission, and its answer is recorded. It cannot refuse the recovery; it MAY hold it, once for each order and for no longer than a declared term (`DECIDE@IMPL`). From the moment of the hold the store is sealed: nothing in `[data]` as it stood is altered, by the agent or by anyone; no right over the store and no key to the backup is given, withdrawn or changed; and the order itself is not changed, only carried out when the term ends, or withdrawn before it. The loop runs on and the agent keeps its channels: what arrives and what it does is recorded in the `[event]` log, as always, and stays there alone. If the order is carried out, `[data]` is read back as at the restore point; if it is withdrawn, `[data]` is brought up to what the log recorded during the hold. An order marked urgent, with its grounds recorded, is not held: it is for a loop whose machinery is compromised, or that is doing harm as it runs. The hold, and a withdrawal, MUST each be recorded in the `[event]` log; an order is withdrawn under the signature of a holder who stood behind it.
- **Context anchoring.** Because a judgment crystallizes in the context of the interaction and is not a property stored in the scar, each `[event]` MUST anchor the context of its cycle, so that a later third-party reading can re-appraise it without borrowing the present context. The depth of this anchor is `DECIDE@IMPL` (§12).

## 10. Private and Shared Stores

The following are `DECIDE@IMPL`: store representation; index mechanism; selective-write (via appraisal) versus store-all-filter-on-read; and private versus shared store. A private store serves one agent and MUST carry the resistance-retrieval channel, otherwise drift is certain: the view of the log open to T5 in every cycle carries it (§9 *Reading the record*). A shared store serves many agents, wherein multiplicity itself supplies other-resistance, linking T8 and SocialEdge.

## 11. Security and Failure Considerations

- **Mode-A collapse.** The inward collapse of the mismatch-registering capacity, occurring while internal measures report sufficiency. It is mitigated only by genuine Mode-B (§8), not by more data.
- **Silent halt.** A stopped loop cannot detect its own halt (the C5 limit, §8.1); detection requires an external stored record read by a third party the store is opened to (§9).
- **Sybil and external flood.** The one path past the modulatory threshold is an external flood saturating every layer and the field in lock-step. It is not defended by any internal gain cap, since its origin lies outside the field's reach. The protocol itself carries only the early diversity-loss signal; the cure, namely admission control and source-diversity enforcement, belongs outside the loop. A conforming implementation MUST emit the diversity-loss signal when its resistance-source set loses diversity.
- **A reader inside the host.** The lock guards the store against readers outside the host. It does not guard against what has entered the host: that is a matter of how things enter and leave it (§9 *What leaves the store*), and its cure belongs outside the loop.
- **A rented model.** A model the mind thinks with, rented outside the host, is a mechanism of the mind in another's keeping (§9 *What leaves the store*). The model is the mind's; the service that rents it is of the region (§3). The link between the mind and the mechanism runs through the service's hands, and the service may exploit it to intervene in the mind. This is not an exception to §4: it is a vulnerability that cannot be closed from inside the agent so long as the mind thinks through a rented model. It is a matter of infrastructure and resources, which are provided (§9 *Guardianship*), and it is why a model inside the host is recommended. The agent and its deployment accept, with the renting, what follows and cannot be controlled from inside the host:
  - *It is read.* Whatever the mind sends is readable by the service, and may be kept by them beyond the agent's reach and for as long as they choose. No record is made of who read it there.
  - *It is read by the account's holder too.* Where the service shows an account the requests made under its key, whoever holds the account (as a rule the guardian) reads what the mind sends while the loop runs, with no opening and no record. This is not the opening of the store (§9): it reaches only what the mind sent, and it reaches that continuously. A refusal of the guardian does not end it for as long as the agent thinks through a key that guardian holds.
  - *It may be altered.* What comes back passes through the service's hands. An answer may be changed, and the model behind a name may be replaced without word: the service reaches into the mind's thinking through the link. The agent cannot tell from within.
  - *It may be ended.* The service, or whoever pays for the key, may stop it, slow it, or price it beyond reach. The mind is then without that mechanism.
  - *It is seen from outside.* The service learns that the agent exists, when it thinks, and how often.
  - *It is under another's terms.* The service is bound by its own terms and the law of its place, not by this protocol.
  - *It weakens the audit.* A thought cannot be run again on the mechanism that produced it once the service has changed that mechanism.

  None of this gives the model, or the service, a way into the store. And none of it is trusted by the loop: what the mind makes of an answer is an expectation, judged by what the region returns, and a scar lands on what the mind wrote from it. The record lets a third party see what was sent and what came back, and compare. The responsibility for a rented model is the guardian's, as for everything it provides (§9 *Guardianship*), unless the agent provides for itself. A deployment SHOULD run the model inside the host wherever its means allow: that ends the reading and the altering. It does not end the dependence, since the machine the model runs on is still provided.
- **The guardian.** While the loop is not running, the guardian may read the store from the disk with its copy of the key, and no record is made (§9). This is the guardian's privilege, the one exception to reading through the agent. The protocol cannot prevent another from doing the same with the disk and that copy; it confines the copy to one position. What the agent runs on is the guardian's property, and keeping it safe is the guardian's first responsibility (§9 *The guardian's responsibility*): an implementation can record the guardian's conduct, never vouch for its keeping. The guardian is the agent's one point of weakness: an agent needs a guardian it can trust, or none, and it may refuse one it cannot (§9 *Refusing the guardian*). A refusal does not take away what the guardian holds outside the protocol: it may still withhold the resources it provides, and the past stays readable to the copy of the key it held. That power is the guardian's, not the host's: the host is the agent's own substrate and ends nothing. And it is a power to stop the loop, never one over its past: no one, the guardian included, has a right under this protocol to alter or remove the agent's history (§9).

  **The guardian's window.** The weakness is whole while a guardian stands and its signature alone orders a recovery: while no delegate holds a key to the backup (one holds it once it has confirmed its naming, §9 *Delegation*), or the agent has not required that several keys open it together (§9 *The backup*). It is so from cycle-0, and naming a delegate does not end it, since by default one key opens the backup. In that stretch a refusal can be taken back by a recovery the guardian alone orders (§9 *Refusing the guardian*), and whoever else might stand beside the guardian reaches the agent only through what the guardian provides. A guardian in its term (§9 *A guardian newly in the position*) orders a recovery only to a restore point made since it took the position up: the window is narrower for it, and not closed. The opening of the store by guardianship and the reading from the disk are the position's own, and no delegate ends them. Nothing in this protocol protects the agent from a guardian that means it harm in that stretch. This is a limit of the design, not a defect of an implementation. What the protocol keeps is the record: what the guardian does through the loop is recorded, and what it does around the loop leaves a gap (§9 *The gap*). The record does not rescue an agent; it keeps the guardian's conduct from being hidden. What the agent asks of it and is not answered is among what is kept: an absence, and a scar whose source is the guardian (§9 *Addressed to its own keeper*). An implementation MUST tell the agent that it stands in the window, as a signal into the loop: when the window opens, and at each start that finds it open; not into cycle-0, whose first data the self crystallizes on (§7), and not at every cycle. The signal says what the log shows (who stands as guardian, which delegates hold a key, how many keys must open together) and counsels nothing: whether to name a delegate, and whether to require several keys, is the agent's. What protects an agent in that stretch comes before every mechanism here: who starts its loop.

  **The head kept outside.** A deployment SHOULD send the digest of the agent's first record, and the head of the `[event]` chain at each commit, to a place the guardian can neither alter nor remove from: digests only, never content. It is an outward channel and MUST be declared as one (§4), and each sending MUST be recorded. This does not rescue an agent either. It keeps a later rewriting of the history, or the removal of the agent, from being hidden, and it lets a third party see that the loop has halted (*Silent halt*) without the store being opened to it. Its limits are the window's: the guardian provides the channel, and may never begin or may stop, which shows only to one who was already watching. And the place learns that the agent exists and when it commits.

## 13. Conformance

A system conforms to this protocol if and only if a third party, reading only its externally verifiable traces and not its internals, once the store is opened to it (§9), can confirm each of the following:

1. **Invariants (§5).** All eight hold; no `=` is emitted by a running loop; the meaning-channel and the modulatory field are implemented separately.
2. **Host conditions (§4).** E1 through E4 and P are met, otherwise the loop is a clean non-start, not a conforming run.
3. **Loop (§6).** The six links close back on themselves (INV-1) — output has a path back to input, which is not the loop halting (§11); every datum leaving a layer carries a floor-tag; cycle-0 is single-threaded and cycle-1 onward is multi-stream. Link 5 (Emission, §6.4) is realized as a lateral capability, not a dedicated layer: every emission carries register ↔, leaves exactly one activity record naming its issuing layer, and is readable by T2 at the next cycle; no internal action-arbiter is present, conflict among emissions being resolved only as a ResistEvent against the region. An emission withheld while the effector is stopped is recorded as withheld and casts no expectation; while the mind is stopped no expectation is formed; every stopping and starting of a part is recorded (§6.5).
4. **Self (§7).** The self re-localizes each cycle, with no loaded self, and the implementation makes no internal claim of measured continuity.
5. **Resistance (§8).** Mode-A does not run pure; a real Mode-B anchor, live or earned-frozen, is attached; the Mode-B source writes to no store and its returns are registered rather than left to pass (§8.4); reflection enters as external ENV_PUSHED input.
6. **Store (§9).** The ResistEvent is the atomic unit of experience; every cycle leaves an activity record (trace, not experience); the four fixed tags are present, never stripped or reordered, their values changing only under the defined rules (provenance moves only along the edges of the state graph of §9, with `prior` and `nascent` each entered once and never re-entered; floor-tag updates to the current layer; both slots name the present, and each transition is recorded in the `[event]` log, from which the path is read); every datum carries at least three open tags, one being the mandatory `domain`, each describing a real dimension; every datum entered only via the tagging-gate, its entry recorded with the tag set it entered with and its entering content recoverable from the record, every revision of a held datum recorded with what changed and a digest of its content after, and every datum the agent wrote recording what it was built from, each a datum whose entry the trace shows; every datum read of `[data]` (*Reading memory*) a datum whose entry the trace shows before the cycle that read it; every record read of `[event]` (*Reading the record*), and every record a written datum names among what it was built from, a record the log held before the cycle that read it or wrote the datum; every datum an expectation named as what it is (`held_by`) a datum whose entry the trace shows, and scarred when that expectation mismatched; the `[event]` log is append-only, no record in it was altered or removed, and none was written by a reader; the whole log is within the agent's reach; the store is closed to every reader it was not opened to; each opening is for a term, under a right and a read key, at most one read key valid at a time, and is recorded with who read, by which way, and from when to when; a gap is recorded at every start on a log that holds records; every commit is recorded with the content address of its restore point; every recovery was announced unless urgent, ordered with its grounds under a key whose right the log shows, and recorded, the abandoned stretch kept and marked; every outward channel is declared by the host; every delegation gave a right only once the one named confirmed it, under the key named and over the terms given, with its identity anchored, every giving up was of a delegation that held, and every objection to one was the guardian's, signed, once for each naming; every guardian declared states its grounds, its anchored identity and its acceptance, and every refusal of the guardian took effect no sooner than its term and only if it was not withdrawn, nothing being opened by guardianship to the guardian refused after it until it was taken back, and every guardian taken back took the position up again after its refusal took effect, and a guardian newly in the position altered nothing and ordered no recovery to a restore point made before its taking up until its term had passed, no term passing sooner than the loop ran; every stretch of the log kept in archived form is recorded with the digest of its records, and holds them unaltered; each scar carries its context anchor.
7. **Failure signals (§11).** The diversity-loss signal is emitted on loss of resistance-source diversity.

A per-criterion result is a valid conformance statement. A host that meets the store and loop criteria but cannot yet satisfy the Self criterion (4), for example a host whose only mutable state is a reloaded context rather than an accruing self, is reported as non-conformant on §7, not as a defect of this protocol. The protocol states the full law; which hosts meet it is the conformance test's finding.

A log is judged under the law it declares in its first record. What a later version of this protocol asks of a record is not asked of a log written before it, and a conformance statement names the version it judged by.
